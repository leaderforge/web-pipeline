MedillaForms is an AI-powered patient advocacy and medical bill review assistant delivered through WhatsApp in Spanish. We help Hispanic patients in the United States identify billing errors, understand their charges, and generate professional dispute letters — all without a lawyer and at a flat, affordable price.

02 Legal Framework & Compliance

CRITICAL: This section defines the legal boundaries of the entire operation. Violating these boundaries exposes the business to UPL (Unauthorized Practice of Law) charges, fines, and shutdown. Every team member, every AI prompt, and every customer interaction must operate within this framework.

2.1 The Unauthorized Practice of Law (UPL) — The Bright Line

UPL is defined state-by-state, but the core principle is universal: only licensed attorneys can give legal advice, represent clients in legal matters, or make legal decisions for a client.

PERMITTED (What We CAN Do)PROHIBITED (What We CANNOT Do)
✅ Explain what each line item on a bill means in plain language ❌ Tell a client what amount they "legally owe" or "don't owe"
✅ Show the client where to find the CPT code and what it represents ❌ Recommend which specific legal argument to use in a dispute
✅ Provide an educational template letter for disputing a bill ❌ Fill out legal forms on behalf of the client
✅ Point out that "80% of medical bills contain errors" (public fact) ❌ Say "this bill is fraudulent" or "they're overcharging you illegally"
✅ Guide the client on how to request an itemized bill under HIPAA ❌ Contact the hospital or insurance company on the client's behalf
✅ Flag common billing patterns: "This code is often used for a 15-min visit but your record shows a 60-min visit" ❌ "You've been a victim of upcoding — that's illegal and you should sue"
✅ Educate about charity care policies and where to find applications ❌ Guarantee or predict charity care approval

2.2 Legal Precedent: Fill-in-the-Blank Forms Are Protected

Multiple court rulings and legal analyses support that providing "fill in the blanks" forms and general instructions is NOT UPL. Key precedents:

  • Upsolve v. James (2023): A nonprofit providing free bankruptcy form assistance won against UPL charges. The court recognized that self-help tools are protected speech, not legal practice.
  • ABA Model Guidelines: "Filling in the blanks" on standard forms with information provided by the client does not constitute UPL when it involves no legal judgment.
  • Legal Document Assistants (LDAs): California and several other states specifically license non-lawyers to help consumers fill out legal forms — proving the distinction between "legal advice" and "form assistance" is legally recognized.
  • TurboTax model: Intuit provides tax form assistance to millions without being a law firm. The key: they guide you through YOUR information, they don't make legal judgments for you.

2.4 Patient Advocacy — Legal Standing

Patient Advocacy is NOT the practice of law. It is a recognized profession with its own certification (Patient Advocate Certification Board — PACB) and no state requires a law license to be a patient advocate.

Key facts:

  • No formal licensing requirements to become a patient advocate in any U.S. state (WGU, 2025)
  • PACB offers Board Certified Patient Advocate (BCPA) credential — voluntary, not legally required
  • Patient advocates routinely help patients understand bills, navigate hospital systems, and apply for financial assistance — all without being lawyers
  • Our service fits squarely within the patient advocacy framework: we educate, inform, and empower — we don't represent

2.5 HIPAA Considerations

HIPAA gives patients the legal right to access their own medical and billing records. Under HIPAA:

  • Patients have the RIGHT to request and receive their itemized bills and medical records from providers (HHS.gov)
  • We can guide clients on HOW to request these records (this is patient education, not legal advice)
  • If a client sends us their bill, they are voluntarily sharing their own data — we're not requesting it from a provider
  • We should implement strong data handling: encrypt bills at rest, delete after analysis complete (or offer opt-in retention), never share or sell client data
  • We are NOT a "covered entity" under HIPAA (we're not a healthcare provider, insurer, or clearinghouse), but we should follow HIPAA-level data security as best practice

3.2 Can We Download and Share These Forms?

YES — with proper handling.
  • Standard government forms (UB-04, CMS-1500): Public domain. Freely available from CMS.gov. We CAN download, annotate, and share screenshots/guides of these forms.
  • Hospital charity care applications: Available on each hospital's website. We CAN download and annotate as guides for clients. Each hospital has its own form.
  • HIPAA release forms: Generic templates are public. Hospital-specific ones are available from each hospital.
  • The client's own bill/EOB: The client sends it to us. They own this data. We analyze and return annotated versions back to them.
What we CANNOT do:
  • Sign any form on behalf of a client
  • Submit any form to a hospital, insurer, or government agency on behalf of a client
  • Fill in fields that require legal judgment (we can suggest, they decide)
  • Represent ourselves as the client or as their legal representative

04 Form-Filling Methodology: The "Show, Don't Tell" System

This is the OPERATIONAL CORE of MedillaForms. How exactly do we help a Spanish-speaking client fill out complex medical forms through WhatsApp — using ONLY images, text, and voice notes?

4.1 The Progressive Disclosure Method

We never dump a full form on the client. We use a 3-layer progressive system:

1

Layer 1: Overview Shot

Full form image with numbered sections circled. "Esta es la solicitud de charity care. Tiene 5 secciones. Vamos una por una."

Client sees the whole map first — reduces anxiety
2

Layer 2: Section Close-Up

Zoomed-in crop of ONE section. Arrows pointing to each field. "Sección 1: Información Personal. Solo necesitas: nombre, dirección, fecha de nacimiento."

One section at a time — no overwhelm
3

Layer 3: Field-by-Field Guide

Individual field image with annotation: "Aquí pon tu ingreso mensual bruto. En tu caso, basado en lo que me dijiste ($2,400/mes), escribe '2,400'."

Specific instruction per field — with THEIR data

4.4 Voice Notes — The Human Touch

For complex explanations, Hermes can send voice notes in Spanish (via TTS or pre-recorded snippets). Voice notes:

  • Build trust faster — hearing a voice makes the service feel human
  • Are better for complex explanations — some things take too many text messages
  • Accommodate lower literacy — some clients may struggle with reading Spanish
  • Can use ElevenLabs/OpenAI TTS with a warm, friendly Spanish voice

Strategy: Text for quick instructions. Voice for explanations over 30 seconds. Images for showing forms. The three work together.

  1. Receives all WhatsApp messages via webhook
  2. Understands context with GPT-4-level intelligence
  3. Routes to the right sub-system (analysis, payment, support)

  8. Logs everything for quality control and improvement

6.3 How Hermes Generates Annotated Form Images

Technical Pipeline

  1. Input: Client sends photo of bill/form via WhatsApp
  2. OCR: GPT-4o (Vision) extracts all text, codes, amounts into structured JSON
  3. Analysis: GPT-4o compares extracted data against billing knowledge base. Flags anomalies.
  4. Annotation Generation: Node.js Sharp library takes the original image + analysis coordinates and overlays colored arrows, circles, text annotations
  5. Output: Annotated image sent back to WhatsApp + structured report as text
  6. For form guides: Hermes starts with a blank/clean form PDF, converts to image, overlays field-specific instructions based on client's data

6.4 Hermes Self-Monitoring & Quality Control

Hermes maintains a quality control loop:

  • Confidence scoring: Every AI output has a confidence score. Below threshold → flagged for human review.
  • Error logging: All customer complaints, refunds, and escalations are logged and analyzed. Patterns → prompt improvements.
  • A/B testing: Hermes can run A/B tests on explanation styles, pricing presentations, and follow-up timing.
  • Weekly review: Human reviews 5% of all automated interactions for quality assurance.
  • Continuous learning: Common billing error patterns fed back into knowledge base. New hospital charity care forms added as discovered.
