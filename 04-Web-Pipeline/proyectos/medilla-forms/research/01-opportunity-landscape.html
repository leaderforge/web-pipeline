1. Executive Summary

We evaluated 5 verticals for AI-powered, WhatsApp-delivered DIY paperwork assistance at a $24–$29 flat price point. Each vertical was scored on 5 dimensions: market demand, competition level, legal risk, profit margin, and AI-fit (how well an LLM can do the job).

TOP PICK: SSDI / SSI Disability Applications 2M Americans apply annually. 65-70% are denied on first attempt. Approval means $1,500+/month for life — so applicants are highly motivated. Zero Spanish-language automated services exist. Expected net: $8K–$15K/month by month 6. Legal risk is manageable with proper disclaimers (non-attorney disability advocates are legal in all 50 states).
RUNNER-UP: IRS Tax Debt & Penalty Relief Huge demand, high-stakes (wage garnishments, liens), excellent AI fit. BUT: tax preparation is more regulated. Requires careful legal review before launching. Keep as expansion #2.

Why This Works

The SSDI/SSI application process is notoriously brutal. The forms are long, the medical evidence requirements are confusing, and 65-70% of initial applications are denied. The appeals process adds more forms, more deadlines, and more complexity. Most people give up or hire a disability attorney who takes 25% of their backpay (capped at $7,200).

An AI-guided DIY approach at $29 solves a massive pain point: applicants get step-by-step help filling out the SSA-16 (disability application), the Adult Function Report (SSA-3373), the Work History Report (SSA-3369), and appeal forms — with explanations in plain language. The AI can even help craft the "disability statement" that's critical for approval.

Why It's #3 (Not #2)

Tax preparation has stricter regulations than disability advocacy. The IRS regulates "tax preparers" and "tax advisors." A DIY guide that fills out Form 843 (Penalty Abatement) or Form 656 (Offer in Compromise) could be classified as tax advice in some interpretations. Needs a legal review before launching. The upside is massive — the tax relief industry is predatory, charging $3K–$5K for work a smart AI guide could do at $29. But the regulatory risk pushes this to expansion #2 rather than immediate launch.

Recommendation: Build MedillaForms + MedillaDisability first. Once those generate stable revenue, hire a tax attorney for a 2-hour consult ($500–$800) to review the IRS DIY assistance concept and validate the disclaimer language. Then launch as "TaxReliefMate" or similar.

5. Rejected Verticals (And Why)

Immigration Forms

HIGH LEGAL RISK — REJECTED. Unauthorized practice of immigration law is a federal crime. Even with disclaimers, the risk of being charged with "holding oneself out as an immigration attorney" is too high. DO NOT pursue this vertical. The $29 price point doesn't justify the legal exposure.

Workers Compensation

Decent demand, but the user already has an attorney in 90% of cases (contingency fee). The DIY space is tiny. Forms are state-specific (50 different systems). Not worth the fragmentation.

SNAP / CalFresh Food Stamps

GetCalFresh.org already provides excellent free multilingual support. The application is relatively simple. Low willingness to pay $29 when free options are good.

Unemployment Benefits (EDD)

Most "help" needed is calling EDD to unblock a claim — an AI can't do that. The forms themselves are simple. Not a good fit for the model.

Student Loan Forgiveness (PSLF)

Free government PSLF Help Tool exists. Numerous nonprofits provide free assistance. Hard to compete with free when the target audience is public servants on modest incomes.

Section 8 Housing Vouchers

Waitlists are closed in most metro areas. Applying is relatively simple when they're open. Demand is high but the "window" to apply is narrow and unpredictable.
